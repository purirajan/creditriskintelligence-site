Why expected loss

A score tells you who is risky. Expected loss tells you what the risk costs.

PD · probability of default
How likely this applicant is to stop paying within 12 months.
LGD · loss given default
If they stop, the share of the money you don't get back.
EAD · exposure at default
How much they will owe at that moment.
ECL · expected credit loss
The three multiplied: the dollars you should expect to lose on this order.

Why not build it yourself?

You can. Building in-house means PD, LGD and exposure models, a rules engine, reason codes that map to adverse-action notices, and a record of every decision, then keeping all of it in step every time your policy changes. Most BNPL risk teams are a handful of people already running the review queue. CreditDrishti gives you that structure from the start, so your team's time goes into the credit policy, not the plumbing around it.

Buyer's checklist

The questions to ask any decisioning vendor, answered

Does it integrate with our systems?
Through a REST API (single or batch) and CSV loan-tape upload, with exports as JSON, CSV or PDF. There are no pre-built connectors to loan origination systems or credit bureaus yet; you send the fields you already collect.
How fast is a decision?
Under 1 millisecond per decision in our local tests. Production latency on your infrastructure has not been benchmarked yet; we would measure it with you.
How are policy changes controlled?
Thresholds are set per product and can be overridden per request, and every decision records the exact thresholds it used. A review-and-approve workflow for policy changes is not built yet.
Is there an audit record for every decision?
Yes. Each decision returns its inputs, policy checks, reasons, thresholds, model ID and version, record ID and timestamp. Today you store that record; hosted storage is on the roadmap.
Can our credit team own it day to day?
Your team sets the thresholds and reason codes without retraining a model. There is no self-serve policy editor yet, so changes are made with us.
How good are the models?
Today's scorecard uses expert-set assumptions and is not yet calibrated to lender data. A backtest calibrates and measures it on your own history before anything goes live.
